Unit 1: The Global Tapestry

Time Period: c. 1200 - c. 1450

Key Concepts: 1.1 Developments in East Asia, 1.2 Developments in Dar al-Islam, 1.3 Developments in South & Southeast Asia, 1.4 State Building in the Americas, 1.5 State Building in Africa, 1.6 Developments in Europe

Overview

Main Theme/Focus: This unit examines the political, social, and cultural characteristics of major civilizations across the world from 1200 to 1450, before the rise of extensive global trade networks. It highlights the diversity of state-building and cultural achievements in a pre-modern world.
Historical Context: Follows the collapse of classical empires (e.g., Rome, Han); sets the stage for Unit 2’s focus on interconnected trade networks by establishing regional foundations.

Major Events & Developments

Event/Development 1: Expansion of the Mongol Empire

 When/Where: c. 1206-1368, Eurasia
 Causes: Genghis Khan’s leadership, superior cavalry tactics
 Effects: Pax Mongolica, safer Silk Roads, spread of technologies (e.g., gunpowder)
 Significance: Created the largest contiguous empire, enhancing cross-continental exchange
